## Wave 3 — The Deepening (2026-10-13)

**The fallback chain, as shipped (CANON §X discipline — technique and fallback both named):**
1. **WebGPU** — a compute shader breathes the 4,200 reckoned motes through the flow field each frame; an instanced render pass draws each as a soft disc, additively. Per-mote tone and a slow breathing of brightness are reckoned from the same seed.
2. **Canvas 2D** — the same reckoned homes as 120 soft sprites in three registers. Fewer, larger, slower: the same dark at a lower resolution.
3. **No script** — the frame's own sky above the written collection; the chamber whole, as at wave 2.
A stage that cannot start — no adapter, a stalled adapter (a 20 s patience, and the record below says why), a device lost mid-flight — hands the sky to the next stage without a word. In calm mode (reduced motion / the `?still` study mode) whichever stage owns the sky draws one settled frame.

**The trial's verdict: PASSES as an enhancement layer; ships as stage one of the chain.** Evidence, all gathered in the fleet's own harness (Playwright headless Chromium 153, this VM):
- *Support reality.* `navigator.gpu` is present in the headless browser; with default flags `requestAdapter()` answers null (no adapter); with the software adapter enabled (`--enable-unsafe-webgpu --use-angle=swiftshader`) an adapter and device answer (vendor `google`, architecture `swiftshader`). On end-user hardware the Chromium browsers ship WebGPU by default; the fleet's other sites should not carry it first regardless (CANON §X: it stays a test-bed resident until a wave verifies it on real hardware — this wave could not, and says so).
- *What the GPU stage adds that the fallback cannot:* 4,200 independently drifting motes advected per frame on the GPU through a two-octave flow field, each with its own tone and glow — a fine-grained depth of field. The Canvas 2D stage reaches the same *sum* (the same reckoned homes, the same registers of colour) with 120 large sprites; it cannot do per-particle flow at that count without taxing the visitor's machine. What the fallback keeps that the GPU stage cannot improve on: the meaning. The stars, the wishes, the Wrong Star, the written collection — untouched by either stage.
- *Initialization verified:* in an instrumented copy of this exact code (harness copy only; the repo carries no instrumentation), the stage completed adapter → device → shader compile → both pipelines → compute + render submission with **zero validation errors** (`uncapturederror` never fired), across repeated runs.
- *The limitation, recorded plainly:* this VM has no GPU, and its software device is destroyed by the platform within seconds of the first frame — `device.lost`, reason `destroyed` — **reproduced with a minimal page holding one triangle and a pinned device**, in both the headless shell and the full Chromium build. Sustained GPU rendering therefore could not be screenshot-verified here; the stage's brightness was set by computed parity with the fallback instead (disc coverage ≈ 10 layers at the tuned size and falloff, against the fallback's measured wash). The archived GPU-launch capture (`screenshots/stars-nebula-gpu.png`) shows the chamber complete under that launch, its wash the chain's settled state. On every observed device death in the real build, the chain caught the sky: the Constellation never rendered without its nebula in any run of any stage.
- *Two defects the trial caught in the trial's own code* (the test bed working as intended): (1) the first WGSL declared the position buffer read-write in a module shared by both stages — a vertex stage may not hold read-write storage; the pipelines are now two modules, the render stage reading what the compute stage writes. (2) An unreferenced WebGPU device is garbage-collected and reports itself lost (`destroyed`); in calm mode, where a single frame is drawn and no loop keeps the closure alive, the device is now pinned to its canvas for the chamber's life. A third finding belongs to the harness, recorded for the next builder: probing a canvas with `getContext('2d')` *sets its mode* and poisons a WebGPU stage not yet configured — stage detection here is by element identity (the fallback replaces the element) and presented frames, never by context probing. Software-adapter latency on this loaded VM measured ~4–7 s for `requestAdapter` alone; the handover patience is 20 s for that reason, and costs the visitor nothing, because stage three is already beneath everything from the first frame.

## Wave 4 — Depth 81: The Second Coda (2026-10-13)

**What was made.** The roadmap's next item, in its own order (items 1, 2 and 4 closed at wave 3): **Depth 81 — the Second Coda**, a room below the Coda Below, reachable only from the Mirror, holding the Cartographer's last fragment. Named in the canon before use, per the discipline: **The Ghost Figure** and **The Second Coda** entered the §III registry, the Master Code gained the gate's line, §V gained planted fact 9, and §VIII's Depth 81 entry stands live. One system, studyable and practicable both; no other chamber was touched.

**The gate — two kept things must agree.** (1) The visitor stands at the eighty-first visit or beyond (the Depths of Return, §II rule 4: 1, 9, 27, 81). (2) **The Ghost Figure is complete**: the Mirror's kept ghost paths — the Ghost Ledger's record of previous visits, up to three paths — laid over one another have together stood in all nine chambers; every seat of the ring touched by at least one ghost. A scholar can reproduce the gate exactly from the `ninth.*` keys alone; a devotee can practice it: return, and walk the chambers your earlier selves have not stood in, until the figure your ghosts trace closes over the whole ring. Only then, descending in the Mirror past the path readout, is the room there. Nothing announces it, before or after (the ration, §XII).

**The fragment.** One question — the only one the Cartographer ever asked outright in all his charting (canon §V, fact 9): *“Will you go back up the slow way, and stand a while in the dark with your kindness still lit, so that a stranger you will never meet can find the first door by it?”* It is not drawn and not chosen — there is one fragment, and the gate is kept state, never chance (the Master Code: no seed, because there is nothing to choose). Darkness is present in it — the dark, the stranger still outside — and does not win: the question's whole weight is a light kept lit for someone else (First Law). ## The Test Bed — first candidates (from wave 1's own set)

The 9th site is the fleet's technique test bed: research feeds new techniques here first; what proves worthwhile graduates to the other eight sites. Wave 1's techniques, assessed as candidates:

1. **Same-document View Transitions** — *first candidate for graduation.* Support: Chromium full; Safari 18+ partial; Firefox behind flag at writing. Why: zero-dependency, degrades to instant change with zero loss, and every fleet site is a multi-view static app that would feel materially better with it. Risk: low.
2. **CSS scroll-driven animations** — *candidate, watch.* Support: Chromium full; Safari 26 shipping; Firefox in development. Why: removes JS scroll handlers fleet-wide (Film's Watchtower and Trend's Live Wire are scroll-heavy). Fallback pattern proven here (IntersectionObserver) is already the fleet-safe half.
3. **`@property` registered custom properties** — *candidate.* Support: Chromium + Safari; Firefox recent. Why: animatable gradients/glows with no JS; pure enhancement, trivially safe fallback.
4. **Speculation Rules prefetch** — *candidate for data-heavy fleet sites.* Support: Chromium. Why: the fleet's sites all fetch same-origin JSON on view changes; prefetch is a one-tag win where supported, inert elsewhere.
5. **Seeded deterministic generation (the Seed Reckoning pattern)** — *candidate as a discipline, not an API:* any fleet feature that varies by day (Trend's dailies, the Vault's rotation) can be made reproducible and testable the same way.
6. **WebGPU** — *trialled at wave 3; still not a candidate.* The Wishfield Nebula proved the degrade-to-meaning chain under every failure the harness could produce, including total device destruction — but the harness has no GPU and destroys its software device within seconds, so the scene is unverified on real hardware. Held at the test bed until a wave verifies it there; the fleet's other sites should not carry it first.
